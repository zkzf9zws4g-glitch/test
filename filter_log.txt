WINDOW
------
Filing dates: 2014-01-01 through 2024-06-30 (inclusive).

DATA SOURCE
-----------
- Filing discovery: SEC EDGAR full-text search API
  (https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index), q='"Form 4"', forms=4,
  dateRange=custom, one calendar day per query (startdt=enddt=<day>).
  This API caps any single query at 10,000 hits (from+size<=10000);
  querying one day at a time (observed ~500-700 Form 4 filings/business
  day) stays comfortably under that cap.
- Transaction data: each filing's primary ownershipDocument XML,
  fetched directly at
  https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/{CIK}/{accession-no-dashes}/{filename}
  (filename taken directly from the full-text-search hit's `_id` field,
  which points at the exact primary document — no separate index.json
  lookup needed). Exhibits attached to the same accession (e.g. powers
  of attorney) are excluded by requiring the search hit's `file_type`
  field == "4" and the filename to end in .xml.
- Market cap: SEC XBRL company facts API,
  https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK{10-digit-padded}.json,
  field dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding (falls back to
  us-gaap:CommonStockSharesOutstanding if absent), most recent value
  with `end` date <= the filing date.
- Exchange: SEC's own https://www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers_exchange.json
  (CIK -> current ticker/exchange), with a documented fallback — see
  "FILTER 6" below.
- Prior purchase history (for the opportunistic screen): each filer's
  own https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK{10-digit-padded}.json, which
  lists their full personal filing history (SEC assigns individual
  CIKs to insiders, independent of which company they filed about).

RATE LIMITING
-------------
All requests go through a single rate-limited client (scripts/common.py)
enforcing a minimum 0.11s between requests (~9 req/s), under SEC's
10 req/s fair-access limit, with a real, identifying User-Agent header
("PeopleSignalPilot research donevtm@gmail.com") and automatic retry
with backoff on 429/403/503.

SAMPLING METHODOLOGY (seed = 42)
---------------------------------
The full population of Form 4 filings in this window is far larger than
can be exhaustively fetched and XML-parsed under the rate limit within
one working session (business-day counts observed in the 500-700/day
range imply >1,000,000 filings across the ~2,700 business days in the
window). So this is a two-stage cluster random sample, not a sample of
the full pre-computed filtered population:

  Stage A — business days (Mon-Fri, 2014-01-01..2024-06-30) are the
  sampling clusters. The full list of business days is shuffled with
  Python's random.Random(42).shuffle() — a single, fixed, reproducible
  seed — which fixes both which days get drawn and the order.

  Stage B — within each sampled day, EVERY Form 4 filing that day is
  examined (a full census within the cluster, not a further subsample),
  so there is no additional within-day selection bias.

  Days are drawn and fully processed one at a time until either (a) the
  pool of filings surviving all 6 filters reaches 52 (a small buffer
  above the 40-50 target), or (b) a hard cap of 160 days is reached.
  This deviates from a literal "filter the full population, then random-
  sample 50" design (infeasible at this scale under SEC's rate limit)
  but is, itself, a genuine random sample — reproducible from the seed
  and this script — not a curated one. No day or filing was ever skipped
  or dropped because of what it contained.

FILTERS APPLIED IN ORDER
-------------------------
1. Transaction code = "P" (open-market purchase), on the non-derivative
   table only, AND not flagged as a Rule 10b5-1 trading-plan transaction.
   10b5-1 detection: the document-level <aff10b5One> checkbox (added to
   the Form 4 XML schema in Feb 2023) when present; for filings that
   predate that schema field, a case-insensitive scan of the filing's
   <footnotes> text for "10b5-1" is used as a fallback signal. This
   fallback will miss a 10b5-1 trade whose footnote never uses that
   exact string, so some pre-2023 10b5-1-scheduled purchases may survive
   into the sample — a known limitation, not something we could fully
   automate away given available data.
2. Transaction value (shares x price-per-share, from that same
   transaction line) >= $250,000.
3. Opportunistic screen: purchase value >= 2x the filer's own median
   prior open-market-purchase value, OR it is their first recorded
   purchase. "Prior" purchase history = that filer's own P-coded
   non-derivative transactions found in up to 20 of their most recent
   Form 4 filings strictly before the current filing date (capped at 20
   for request-volume reasons — a filer with an very long, sparse
   purchase history could in principle have an older purchase that
   falls outside this window and isn't counted in the median; this is a
   documented approximation, not a full-lifetime history in every case).
4. Filer role: reportingOwnerRelationship isDirector=1 OR isOfficer=1
   (a filer who is ONLY a 10%-owner, with both of those false, is
   excluded, per the task's explicit instruction).
5. Issuer market cap between $200M and $20B at the filing date.
   Market cap = shares outstanding (as of the nearest company-facts
   date <= filing date) x the transaction's own price-per-share. No
   independent market-data API was reachable from this environment
   (stooq.com returned a JS/bot-check page, not data) — the insider's
   own reported execution price for an open-market purchase is used as
   the filing-date price proxy instead. This is a reasonable but
   documented approximation, not an independently-sourced closing price.
6. Exchange: NYSE or Nasdaq only (OTC/pink sheets excluded). Primary
   check is SEC's own company_tickers_exchange.json. IMPORTANT: that
   file is a CURRENT-DAY snapshot of active listings, so any issuer
   that has since been delisted, gone bankrupt, or been acquired would
   silently disappear from it even if it was legitimately NYSE/Nasdaq-
   listed at its filing date — which would survivorship-bias the sample
   toward companies still trading today. Two real examples hit during
   development: Revlon Inc (NYSE:REV, delisted 2022 bankruptcy) and Ion
   Geophysical Corp (NYSE:IO, delisted 2022 bankruptcy) are both absent
   from the current file. Fallback used when an issuer isn't in the
   current file: the filing's own SEC Exchange Act file number (from
   the full-text-search hit metadata, no extra request needed) — a
   "001-" prefix means the issuer was registered under Exchange Act
   Section 12(b) (listed on a national securities exchange) AT FILING
   TIME; "000-"/absent means Section 12(g) (OTC/unlisted). This
   correctly recovers legitimately-NYSE-listed-at-the-time issuers like
   Revlon, but can't distinguish NYSE vs Nasdaq vs a minor exchange
   (e.g. NYSE American) specifically for delisted issuers — those rows'
   `exchange` field is written as "NYSE/Nasdaq (inferred, not in
   current listing file)" rather than a specific hard claim, so this
   uncertainty is visible in the output rather than hidden.

FILTER NOT AUTOMATED (BY DESIGN)
----------------------------------
Per instructions, "was this person a proven founder/operator" was NOT
attempted — no automated or heuristic scoring of operator quality was
applied. That is an explicitly manual, later step.

COLUMN NOTE: "cik"
-------------------
The output CSV's `cik` column is the FILER's (reporting owner's) own
SEC CIK, not the issuer's — chosen because it's the more useful key for
the manual follow-up research step (looking up that person's other
filings), and because issuer identity is already fully captured by
`issuer_name` + `ticker` + `source_filing_url`.
